## News

[DataDome publishes analysis of "buy for me" AI shopping agents](https://datadome.co/agent-trust-management/how-buy-for-me-ai-agents-are-locking-up-inventory/) and their inventory-locking and price-scraping risks to online retailers. The report frames autonomous shopping agents—including [OpenAI Operator, Perplexity shopping features, and Google AI Mode](https://datadome.co/agent-trust-management/how-buy-for-me-ai-agents-are-locking-up-inventory/)—as generating bot-like traffic patterns with legitimate authorization, creating ambiguity between authorized deal-hunting and fraud. DataDome emphasizes that [80% of AI agents don't properly identify themselves](https://datadome.co/threat-research/ai-agent-identity-crisis/) and positions its Agent Trust control plane as the solution, arguing that traditional bot detection (which distinguishes humans from non-humans) cannot distinguish authorized agents from malicious ones operating under stolen or spoofed credentials.

## Why it matters

This analysis positions DataDome's recently launched [Agent Trust control plane](https://datadome.co/products/agent-trust-management/) and [Priority Protect virtual waiting room](https://datadome.co/products/priority-protect/) as essential infrastructure for managing agentic commerce risk—a continuation of its May 2026 positioning (see [May 13](https://datadome.co/agent-trust-management/how-buy-for-me-ai-agents-are-locking-up-inventory/), [May 11](https://datadome.co/agent-trust-management/how-buy-for-me-ai-agents-are-locking-up-inventory/) items). The core message—that intent assessment throughout the user journey, not identity verification alone, is required—extends DataDome's narrative arc and reinforces vendor differentiation around behavioral + identity-based controls. For retailers and payment processors, the implication is significant: agentic commerce liability and cart-stage fraud (not just checkout fraud) now require pre-transaction monitoring. The report also surfaces a regulatory gap: there is currently no agreed-upon framework in card network rules for dispute resolution when autonomous agents execute transactions, suggesting forthcoming payment rail governance.
