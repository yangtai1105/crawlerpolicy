Here's a compact digest of the most important distinct regulatory actions concerning AI training data, AI crawlers, content licensing, or generative AI and copyright from the last 30 days:

### European Union

1.  **EU AI Act Transparency and General-Purpose AI (GPAI) Obligations Become Enforceable**
    As of August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office and national competent authorities began enforcing key provisions of the EU AI Act. This phase introduces mandatory transparency requirements, such as notifying users when they are interacting with an AI system and labeling deepfakes and AI-generated content with machine-readable marks. Additionally, providers of General-Purpose AI (GPAI) models are now subject to substantive obligations, including documenting technical information, implementing copyright policies, and publishing detailed summaries of their training content.
    Source: European Commission - Shaping Europe's digital future (The enforcement framework of the AI Act)

2.  **EU AI Office Initiates Enforcement Actions on Copyright Compliance for AI Training Data**
    In August 2026, the EU's AI enforcers, specifically the European Commission, contacted over 30 AI companies to request details on their compliance with European copyright rules. Under the AI Act, providers of training models are required to provide a summary of the data used for developing their technology. This action targets companies that have made insufficient disclosures or have not responded to informal inquiries regarding copyright compliance.
    Source: Euractiv - EXCLUSIVE: EU orders leading AI labs to detail security practices

### United States

1.  **US FTC Proposes Enforcement Policy Statement on Personalized Pricing**
    On August 19, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) published for public comment a proposed Enforcement Policy Statement concerning "personalized pricing," also referred to as "surveillance pricing." This policy statement indicates that the FTC may pursue enforcement actions against companies that use consumer data, potentially including AI-driven tools, to set individualized prices without adequate transparency. The public has 30 days to submit comments on this proposed policy.
    Source: King & Spalding - FTC Proposes Enforcement Framework for “Personalized Pricing” Amid Growing Federal and State Scrutiny
