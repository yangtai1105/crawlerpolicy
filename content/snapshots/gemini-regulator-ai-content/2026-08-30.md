Here's a compact digest of the most important distinct regulatory actions and legislative proposals concerning AI training data, AI crawlers, content licensing, or generative AI and copyright from the last 30 days (July 31, 2026, to August 30, 2026):

1.  **EU AI Act Transparency and General-Purpose AI (GPAI) Enforcement Begins**
    As of August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office and national authorities commenced enforcement of key provisions of the EU AI Act. This includes mandatory transparency requirements for AI systems interacting with individuals, clear labeling for deepfakes and AI-generated content, and obligations for GPAI model providers to document technical information, implement copyright policies, and publish detailed summaries of their training content. The EU AI Office now possesses formal investigative and enforcement powers, including the ability to impose substantial fines for non-compliance.

2.  **EU AI Office Inquires into AI Companies' Copyright Compliance for Training Data**
    The EU's AI enforcers have contacted over 30 AI companies, requesting detailed information on how they are complying with European copyright rules, specifically regarding the summaries of data used to train their models. This action directly addresses the transparency obligations related to AI training data and copyright under the newly enforced AI Act.

3.  **UK CMA Imposes AI Opt-Out and Attribution Requirements on Google**
    On August 1, 2026, the UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) issued a Publisher Conduct Requirement to Google. This legally binding rule mandates that Google allow publishers to opt out of having their content used for AI training, fine-tuning, and for the "grounding" of AI Overviews and AI Mode answers. Google is also required to provide clearer attribution links in AI-generated results.

4.  **US FTC Proposes Policy Statement on Suppression of Accuracy in AI Systems**
    The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) released a Proposed Policy Statement on July 1, 2026, addressing concerns that AI companies might steer AI system outputs in ways that contradict reasonable consumer expectations for objectivity and accuracy. The FTC indicated that such actions could constitute deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The public comment period for this proposal closed on July 31, 2026.

5.  **US FTC Proposes Enforcement Policy on Personalized Pricing**
    On August 19, 2026, the US FTC announced a proposed Enforcement Policy Statement on Personalized Pricing, opening a 30-day public comment period. While not exclusively focused on AI, this proposal signals increased regulatory scrutiny of businesses that use consumer data, including through AI and predictive analytics, to offer personalized prices without clear and conspicuous disclosures, potentially violating Section 5 of the FTC Act.

6.  **US Representative Introduces AI Advertising Disclosure Act**
    On August 25, 2026, U.S. Representative Seth Magaziner introduced the AI Advertising Disclosure Act. This proposed legislation aims to require AI chatbots and generative search engines to clearly disclose when their responses or recommendations are influenced by paid commercial arrangements. The bill seeks to ensure transparency in AI-generated content that may be affected by commercial interests.
