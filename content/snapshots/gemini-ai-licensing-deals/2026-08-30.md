Here is a compact digest of important developments in AI training data licensing deals, content partnerships, and lawsuits from the last 14 days:

1.  **Round Hill Music Sues Suno and Anthropic for Copyright Infringement**
    Independent music publisher Round Hill Music filed two separate copyright lawsuits on August 17, 2026, against AI firms Suno and Anthropic. The lawsuits allege that both companies illegally trained their AI models on Round Hill's copyrighted music and lyrics, including popular songs, by scraping content from the open web without permission or license. Round Hill is seeking over $1 billion in damages and has also accused Suno of Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) violations, naming data-scraping firm Bright Data as a co-defendant in the Suno suit.

2.  **Brazilian Newspaper Folha Settles Lawsuit with OpenAI and Signs Deal with Google**
    Brazilian newspaper Folha has settled its copyright lawsuit against OpenAI, the creator of ChatGPT, by entering into an agreement with the AI company. This settlement follows closely on the heels of Folha announcing a separate content licensing deal with Google. The specifics of both agreements were not immediately disclosed, but they represent a trend of publishers seeking compensation and control over their content used in AI training.

3.  **Microsoft Secures Content Deal with Australia's Nine Entertainment**
    Microsoft has signed its first content licensing deal for news in the Asia Pacific region with Australia's Nine Entertainment. This partnership allows Microsoft to access Nine Entertainment's content, likely for training its AI models and enhancing its AI-powered services. The terms of the agreement were not publicly disclosed.

4.  **Judge Largely Rejects Perplexity's Motion to Dismiss Reddit's Copyright Lawsuit**
    A federal judge on July 31, 2026, largely rejected an attempt by Perplexity and SerpApi to dismiss a copyright lawsuit filed by Reddit. Reddit's lawsuit, originally filed in October of the previous year, accuses Perplexity and three data-scraping suppliers (SerpApi, Oxylabs, and AWMProxy) of "industrial-scale" bypassing of technical protections to obtain Reddit content for Perplexity's AI answer engine. The judge's ruling indicates that Reddit has plausibly argued that Perplexity conspired with at least one of the data scrapers to bypass access controls and acquire Reddit's material.
