## Summary

Penske Media and Chegg have filed an appeal after a court dismissed their lawsuit against Google over AI Overviews. The dismissed suit alleged that the AI-generated search summaries damaged their traffic and revenue. The court originally tossed the claims on grounds that no formal bargain existed between the publishers and Google.

## Key facts

- Penske Media and Chegg filed an appeal following the dismissal of their lawsuit against Google.
- The lawsuit alleged that Google's AI Overviews harmed the companies' traffic and revenue.
- The court originally dismissed the lawsuit after citing that no formal bargain had been struck with Google.

## Insight

Publishers challenging search engines over AI summaries face significant judicial resistance when attempting to claim breach of implied contract or economic entitlement in the absence of a binding, formal commercial agreement.

## Implication

If appellate judges uphold the dismissal, web publishers will have fewer legal avenues under traditional contract theories to challenge zero-click AI summaries without statutory or antitrust intervention.

## Why it matters

AI search features increasingly answer user queries directly, reducing referral traffic without direct publisher compensation, making legal clarity around publisher-platform obligations critical for digital media economics.
