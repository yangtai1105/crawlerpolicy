## Summary

The News/Media Alliance has filed an amicus brief in the multi-district litigation brought by over a dozen news publishers against OpenAI and Microsoft. The underlying lawsuit alleges that the tech companies utilized publisher content without authorization to train artificial intelligence models and deliver real-time AI responses.

## Key facts

- The News/Media Alliance filed an amicus brief in multi-district litigation against OpenAI and Microsoft.
- Over a dozen news publishers have sued OpenAI and Microsoft in the consolidated action.
- The litigation claims OpenAI and Microsoft used publisher content without permission to train AI systems.
- The lawsuits also allege unauthorized use of publisher content to power real-time AI answers.

## Insight

Publishing trade organizations are coordinating legal pressure via amicus filings to contest both offline training uses and live inference retrieval by AI developers.

## Implication

OpenAI and Microsoft face unified publisher opposition in consolidated litigation that could shape legal standards for unauthorized web scraping for model training and search synthesis.

## Why it matters

The case targets two foundational use cases of scraped editorial content—model pre-training and real-time retrieval—potentially altering legal liabilities for web-crawled content across the AI sector.
