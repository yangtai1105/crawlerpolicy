## Summary

In its November 2026 Market Overview for Bot Protection and Management Solutions, Gartner categorized bot defense as a distinct market rather than an integrated WAAP feature, driven by the emergence of 'gray bots' and autonomous AI agents. Gartner projects that 80% of enterprises with critical public-facing web applications will deploy dedicated bot management owned by cybersecurity teams by 2028. In response, bot management vendors such as DataDome are shifting enforcement from binary bot-or-human detection to dynamic, intent-based behavioral scoring.

## Key facts

- Gartner published its report, [Market Overview for Bot Protection and Management Solutions](https://www.gartner.com/document-reader/document/8600637?ref=solrAll&refval=591090977&), on 4 November 2026.
- Gartner stated that organizations are seeking comprehensive visibility and mitigation, 'reestablishing bot protection and management as a distinct market rather than a stand-alone feature.'
- Gartner identified 'a new category of "gray bots" that cannot be clearly classified as good or bad without evaluating their intention and business relevance.'
- Gartner estimated that by 2028, '80% of enterprises with critical public-facing web applications will adopt a bot protection and management solution owned primarily by the cybersecurity team.'
- Gartner noted buyers must choose between 'bundled WAAP platform add-ons for basic threat mitigation and stand-alone solutions/offerings to gain the deep visibility and policy control needed for complex governance.'
- DataDome's State of Bot & Agent Security Report 2026 reported that bad bot traffic grew 124% over 12 months, exceeding human traffic growth by more than 9x.
- DataDome introduced an 'Agent Trust' system that issues every agent a dynamic 100-point behavioral score across websites, mobile apps, APIs, and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.

## Insight

The growth of delegated AI agents that research, shop, and compare prices has introduced 'gray bots'—traffic that cannot be classified as good or bad by user-agent string or IP identity alone. Consequently, bot defense is shifting from edge gating to real-time, behavioral intent scoring that spans websites, APIs, and MCP servers.

## Implication

Security, fraud, and e-commerce teams must define intent-based traffic policies rather than default-blocking automation, as blunt filtering risks blocking purchasing agents acting on behalf of real human customers.

## Why it matters

Autonomous agents represent an increasing share of non-human web activity. If security controls cannot distinguish opportunistic scrapers from delegated consumer agents, websites risk either absorbing credential and inventory abuse or severing access to legitimate agent-driven revenue.
