## Summary

A second Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) disclosure has been recorded against draft-mckenna-webbotauth-collection-attestation-02 in the IETF Web Bot Auth Working Group. In the same tracker update, draft-popov-webbotauth-semantic-anchor-00 was flagged as expiring soon following six months without an updated revision.

## Key facts

- A second IPR disclosure was filed on draft-mckenna-webbotauth-collection-attestation-02, bringing its total disclosure count from 1 to 2.
- The McKenna draft, titled 'Attested Conduct for Anonymous Web Data Collection: Use Cases, Requirements, and a Tiered Model', was published on 2026-11-17.
- draft-popov-webbotauth-semantic-anchor-00, titled 'Identity Anchor for Domain-Root AI Discovery (Semantic Anchor)' and dated 2026-06-05, transitioned to 'Expires soon' status.
- The Web Bot Auth working group currently tracks one adopted WG document, draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol-01, and 14 related Internet-Drafts.

## Insight

IPR disclosures signal potential patent claims or proprietary encumbrances over emerging crawler verification frameworks, introducing licensing considerations into what is intended to be an open standard for anonymous data collection conduct.

## Implication

Working group participants and prospective implementers of anonymous collection attestation will need to review the disclosed terms to evaluate whether royalty-free or specific licensing conditions apply to the protocol before adoption.

## Why it matters

Patent disclosures within standard tracks can alter the viability of open-source and cross-industry crawler authentication tooling if essential claims carry restrictive or commercial licensing conditions.
