## Summary

According to reporting from The Verge, Meta has removed terms of service language that legal and industry experts interpreted as allowing the scraping of third-party news sites for AI model training. The revision follows scrutiny over the company's data collection practices regarding external publisher content. Meta's updated legal terms alter the framework governing how web sources may be accessed or ingested for training purposes.

## Insight

Platform terms of service are increasingly treated as contested legal ground for AI training boundaries, where contractual ambiguity can trigger pushback from publishers and regulatory observers before any crawler directive is even evaluated.

## Implication

News publishers may gain clearer legal leverage against automated ingest by Meta's systems, while Meta will likely need to rely more explicitly on standard crawler controls, commercial licenses, or opt-out registries for AI data acquisition.

## Why it matters

Clarifying whether terms of service implicitly authorize web-scale scraping is critical for the evolving boundary between copyright enforcement, publisher access controls, and AI training pipelines.
