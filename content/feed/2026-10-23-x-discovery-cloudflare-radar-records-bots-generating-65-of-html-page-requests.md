## Summary

Cloudflare Radar 7-day data shows automated bots account for 65% of HTML page requests, leaving human visitors at 35%. Overall, bot activity accounts for more than 50% of all HTTP requests across the network. The findings reflect the growing operational and financial burden of AI crawlers and automated agents on web publishers.

## Key facts

- Cloudflare Radar 7-day data indicates that bots generate 65% of HTML page requests, with humans accounting for 35%.
- Bots comprise over 50% of all HTTP requests overall in the measured period.
- The findings underscore growing AI crawler and agent traffic impacts on publisher economics, infrastructure costs, and referral value.

## Insight

Machine traffic has crossed the threshold into becoming the primary consumer of rendered web content, with automated requests now outpacing human visits nearly two-to-one for HTML assets.

## Implication

Publishers and infrastructure providers will likely accelerate aggressive bot-management, gating, and commercial crawler access controls to mitigate hosting costs unbacked by referral value.

## Why it matters

When nearly two-thirds of HTML requests stem from machines rather than humans, traditional ad-supported and human-centric monetization models face severe economic divergence from infrastructure costs.
