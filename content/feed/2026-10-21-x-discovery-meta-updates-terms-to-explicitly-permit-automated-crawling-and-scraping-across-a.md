## Summary

Meta has revised its terms of service to explicitly allow both the company and third parties acting on its behalf to deploy automated software across associated domains. The revision covers technologies such as web scrapers, bots, crawlers, and user agents. Under the revised terms, these automated systems may access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl data to support Meta's products and services.

## Key facts

- Meta revised its terms of service governing automated access across associated domains.
- The terms authorize automated access by Meta itself as well as entities acting on its behalf.
- Covered tools explicitly include automated software such as web scrapers, bots, crawlers, and user agents.
- Permitted actions include accessing, indexing, caching, analyzing, collecting, retrieving, or crawling associated domains.
- The data collection and processing activities are designated for Meta's products and services.

## Insight

Platform terms of service are increasingly adapting legal language to provide explicit contractual authorization for automated data ingestion, bridging the gap between platform operations and web scraping for model training and indexing.

## Implication

Partners, hosted domains, and ecosystem participants operating on or connected to Meta's domains can expect automated crawlers and scrapers operating on Meta's behalf with formal terms-level authorization.

## Why it matters

Explicit contractual permission to crawl and scrape protects platform operators from breach-of-contract claims and establishes a clear legal foundation for broad automated data collection.
