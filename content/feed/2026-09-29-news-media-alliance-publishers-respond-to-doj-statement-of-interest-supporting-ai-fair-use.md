## Summary

The New York Times and other publishers filed a response opposing a Statement of Interest submitted by the U.S. Department of Justice in litigation involving Microsoft and OpenAI. The federal government's filing argued that copying content for artificial intelligence model training qualifies as fair use. News publishers strongly contest this stance as they seek legal recognition of their intellectual property rights against unauthorized AI training.

## Insight

The intervention of the Department of Justice in support of AI model developers elevates copyright litigation into a broader conflict over federal policy, challenging traditional media monetization models by framing comprehensive web scraping for AI training under broad fair use protections.

## Implication

If federal courts adopt the DOJ position on fair use, publishers and content creators will face significantly diminished legal leverage to secure bilateral training licensing deals with major AI developers.

## Why it matters

The outcome of this dispute could determine whether machine access for training generative AI systems remains a commercial licensing market or becomes an uncompensated fair use baseline across the entire web.
