## Summary

Google has expanded its News AI pilot program to more than 400 publications globally, incorporating local U.S. news outlets alongside major national and international media. The licensing initiative pays publishers to supply content for experimental features such as AI-generated story previews and customizable audio briefings. Google confirmed the program does not compensate publishers via a pay-per-value model, distinguishing it from separate licensing arrangements for search and Gemini surfaces.

## Key facts

- Google expanded its News AI pilot program to over 400 publications worldwide, adding outlets such as CNN, The Financial Times, Axios, Forbes, The Post and Courier, Oil City News, Shaw Media, and The Texas Tribune.
- The pilot initially launched in December with international partners including The Guardian, El País, The Times of India, and The Washington Post.
- Google spokesperson Brianna Puccini Duff stated: "People’s information consumption habits are changing, and we want to make sure publishers of all sizes, local newsrooms included, can navigate these shifts."
- Google declined to provide specific financial terms but confirmed the News AI pilot does not operate on a "pay-per-value" model.
- The parallel "pay-per-value" framework compensates publishers based on how content contributes to outputs across AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Gemini.
- Participating pilot publishers provide content for experimental features including "story previews" (AI article summaries with accuracy disclaimers) and "audio briefings" (customizable synthetic podcasts with citation links).

## Insight

By separating product-specific AI licensing pilots from its broader pay-per-value generative search initiatives, Google is establishing distinct commercial tiers that pay flat participation rates for experimental features rather than usage-based attribution royalties.

## Implication

Publishers negotiating licensing frameworks must evaluate multiple siloed agreements across distinct Google surfaces, weighing fixed testing stipends against variable attribution-driven payouts.

## Why it matters

The rapid expansion of AI summarization pilots demonstrates how platform aggregators are attempting to preempt publisher copyright disputes and search traffic degradation by establishing direct licensing channels for generative outputs.
