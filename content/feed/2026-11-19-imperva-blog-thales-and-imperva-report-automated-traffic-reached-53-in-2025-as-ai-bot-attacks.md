## Summary

According to findings from the 2026 Thales Bad Bot Report, automated traffic comprised 53% of all observed web traffic in 2025, with malicious bots representing 40% of the total. AI-enabled bot attacks surged 12.5-fold year over year, while 27% of bot attacks specifically targeted APIs. The emergence of autonomous AI agents interacting directly with APIs is blurring technical distinctions between legitimate automated transactions and malicious abuse.

## Key facts

- Automated traffic accounted for 53% of observed web traffic in 2025, with malicious bots making up 40% of total traffic according to the [2026 Thales Bad Bot Report](https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/resources/application-security/2026-bad-bot-report).
- AI-enabled bot attacks increased 12.5-fold year over year.
- Thales blocked 17.2 trillion bad bot requests across its network in 2025.
- The 2026 Bad Bot Report recorded that 27% of bot attacks targeted APIs and 21% targeted application business logic.
- An analysis of more than 32,000 API endpoints in the [2026 API Security Token & Authentication Risk Report](https://www.imperva.com/resources/resource-library/reports/imperva-api-security-token-authentication-risk-report/?6) revealed that nearly 40% exhibited multiple authentication-related risks.
- The [2026 Thales Data Threat Report](https://cpl.thalesgroup.com/data-threat-report) found that 61% of surveyed organizations reported their AI applications were being targeted.

## Insight

Automated traffic classification can no longer rely on binary bot-versus-human detection because authorized agentic AI workflows structurally mimic scraping, high-frequency querying, and automated transactions.

## Implication

Security teams and web operators will have to pivot access controls away from blanket volumetric or signature bot mitigation toward evaluating user intent, business logic validity, and API authorization scopes.

## Why it matters

With automated traffic now representing the majority of web requests, standard bot defenses risk either disrupting revenue-generating AI agents or admitting sophisticated AI-driven credential and business-logic attacks.
