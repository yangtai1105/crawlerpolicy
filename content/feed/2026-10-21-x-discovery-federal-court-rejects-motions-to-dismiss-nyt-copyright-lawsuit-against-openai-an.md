## Summary

A federal court has denied motions to dismiss The New York Times' copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, allowing the case to move forward. The court cited evidence showing that large language models were capable of reproducing full copyrighted articles verbatim. The decision keeps critical legal questions surrounding AI training data and model outputs active in federal court.

## Key facts

- A federal court ruled that The New York Times copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft must proceed.
- The court rejected motions to dismiss filed in the case.
- The ruling cited evidence of large language models outputting full copyrighted articles.

## Insight

Judicial scrutiny is focusing directly on verbatim output generation as sufficient ground to sustain copyright infringement claims against AI developers past the motion-to-dismiss threshold.

## Implication

OpenAI and Microsoft will be forced into deeper discovery regarding their training datasets, scraping methods, and memorization mitigations, potentially exposing internal data ingestion pipelines to public scrutiny.

## Why it matters

A full trial or extended discovery phase creates substantial legal exposure for generative AI developers and may establish precedent defining whether unauthorized content ingestion and memorization constitute direct copyright infringement.
