## Summary

Independent nonprofit outlet The Record North Shore filed a copyright infringement lawsuit in U.S. District Court against Surround Sound Media and its founder Robert Kellman. The complaint alleges that Surround Sound's North Shore Weekly systematically republishes original local reporting and photography across its automated network of 175 local news sites. The suit seeks an injunction, the removal of infringing content, and monetary damages of up to $25,000 per incident.

## Key facts

- The Record North Shore filed a copyright infringement lawsuit in U.S. District Court against Surround Sound Media and founder Robert Kellman.
- Surround Sound Media uses artificial intelligence to operate approximately 175 local news outlets across the United States.
- The lawsuit claims Surround Sound's North Shore Weekly 'systematically and improperly republished The Record’s original reporting and photography.'
- The Record seeks an injunction preventing future infringement, the removal of copyrighted materials, and damages of up to $25,000 per incident.
- The Record editor-in-chief Joe Coughlin stated: 'We allege that that work was used inappropriately and without authorization. Those alleged actions are a threat to the work itself, to the work of small publishers, and to the community.'
- Surround Sound Media stated that 'AI tools help monitor public records, identify developments and prepare drafts, which journalists check against source material, edit and put into context before publication.'
- The Record North Shore, founded in 2020, employs four full-time staff members and generated $440,000 in revenue in 2025.

## Insight

The litigation highlights the legal boundary between unprotected factual reporting and actionable infringement in AI content networks, focusing on the reuse of original photography and systematic text reproduction by automated aggregation pipelines.

## Implication

Automated local publishing operations that rely on AI to ingest, summarize, or republish regional reporting face direct legal exposure, particularly when their workflows ingest original creative assets like photojournalism.

## Why it matters

This case opens an active legal front between resource-constrained local newsrooms and scalable AI content networks that extract value from community reporting without licensing agreements or consent.
