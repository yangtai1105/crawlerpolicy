## Summary

During its Q3 earnings call, DoorDash disclosed it has received only several hundred orders total from third-party AI agents such as Muse, Instinct, and Fo, compared to its daily average of 3 million deliveries. CEO Tony Xu stated the company will not fight technological gravity and intends to build relationships with third-party agent developers rather than block them. DoorDash has also deployed internal agent interfaces, including a Model Context Protocol integration for corporate chatbot ordering.

## Key facts

- DoorDash revealed it has processed only 'several hundred' total orders placed through third-party AI agents like Muse, Instinct, and Fo.
- DoorDash averages 3 million deliveries daily according to its 2025 report, making agent traffic a tiny fraction of total activity.
- CEO Tony Xu stated: 'my take on all things technology is you never want to fight gravity. But at the same time, you don’t want to be delusional.'
- Xu noted that users still predominantly place orders through DoorDash's app, but the company sees potential future opportunities to advertise inside third-party AI agents.
- DoorDash has deployed proprietary agent tools: an Ask DoorDash chatbot (June), a CLI agent (July), a text-to-order agent (September), and an MCP server for corporate ordering from chatbots.
- The broader commerce landscape is splitting on delegated agent traffic, with Amazon blocking personal agents while Walmart and DoorDash accommodate them.

## Insight

Platform responses to delegated agentic commerce are diverging sharply between defensive blocking and partnership exploration, with DoorDash treating early agent traffic as a nascent advertising and distribution channel rather than hostile scraping.

## Implication

Third-party consumer and corporate AI agents will retain API and programmatic purchase access on DoorDash, contrasting with strict blockades from competitors like Amazon, potentially driving agent developers toward participating merchant platforms.

## Why it matters

Real-world transaction volume from agentic AI remains negligible relative to mainstream consumer commerce, grounding speculative market hype in actual order counts while platforms establish their foundational access policies.
