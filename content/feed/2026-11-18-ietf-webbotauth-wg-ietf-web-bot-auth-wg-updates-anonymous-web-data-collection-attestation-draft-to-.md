## Summary

The IETF Web Bot Auth Working Group published revision -02 of the Internet-Draft on attested conduct for anonymous web data collection. Authored under draft-mckenna-webbotauth-collection-attestation-02, the updated document expands to 14 pages and records an associated intellectual property disclosure. The proposal outlines use cases, requirements, and a tiered model for verifying crawler conduct without disclosing operator identity.

## Key facts

- The IETF Web Bot Auth Working Group tracker updated 'draft-mckenna-webbotauth-collection-attestation' from revision -00 to -02.
- The document is titled 'Attested Conduct for Anonymous Web Data Collection: Use Cases, Requirements, and a Tiered Model'.
- The draft page count increased from 13 pages to 14 pages with a publication date of November 17, 2026.
- An Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) disclosure count of 1 was registered against the draft in the working group index.
- The document remains categorized under related Internet-Drafts with status 'New' and 'I-D Exists' on the [Web Bot Auth Working Group tracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/webbotauth/).

## Insight

Rapid iteration from revision -00 to -02 alongside a new IPR disclosure indicates accelerating formalization and proprietary or patented stakes in privacy-preserving, conduct-based crawler verification models.

## Implication

Web crawlers seeking to avoid indiscriminate blocking while preserving operator anonymity may soon face standardized cryptographic attestation frameworks that balance behavioral compliance against potential patent licensing conditions.

## Why it matters

As web publishers implement stricter perimeter defenses against automated scraping, standardized mechanisms that verify crawler adherence to acceptable conduct without unmasking competitive operations represent an alternative to binary IP blocking.
