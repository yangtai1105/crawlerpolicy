## Summary

A survey of 100 marketing and security executives conducted by HUMAN Security found that over 94% believe AI agents are visiting their digital properties, yet only 16% of marketing leaders and 4% of CISOs can quantify that traffic. Furthermore, 54% of CISOs lack confidence in their tools' ability to separate malicious bots from legitimate AI agents. The operational friction is already apparent, with 58% of CMOs reporting broken attribution tracking and 44% of CISOs noting an increase in automated scraping against APIs and applications.

## Key facts

- HUMAN surveyed 100 leaders in 2026, comprising 50 marketing executives (CMO/VP) and 50 CISOs across mid-market and enterprise companies.
- 94% of marketing leaders and 98% of CISOs believe AI agents are possibly or definitely present on their sites and applications.
- Only 16% of marketing leaders and 4% of CISOs possess the capability to quantify AI agent traffic.
- 54% of CISOs and 40% of marketing leaders are not very confident or not at all confident that their current tooling can distinguish malicious bots from legitimate AI agents.
- 48% of CISOs and 30% of marketing leaders state their organizations do not actively filter non-human traffic at all.
- 58% of marketing leaders reported broken attribution tracking, and 52% reported distorted CAC and LTV calculations over the past 18 months due to automated traffic.
- 44% of CISOs cited growth in automated scraping against applications or APIs, and 42% cited account takeover attacks.
- 44% of marketing leaders would significantly reallocate budget upon discovering low-quality or non-human traffic, compared to only 8% of CISOs who would respond significantly.

## Insight

An organizational split exists between security and marketing over agentic web traffic: CMOs view unmeasured AI agents as economic waste causing distorted CAC and broken attribution, while CISOs categorize automated traffic primarily as security threats like scraping and account takeover, leaving both sides without telemetry to discern beneficial autonomous agents from malicious bots.

## Implication

Enterprises that fail to deploy unified classification frameworks will face internal friction between marketing teams wanting to court AI buyers and security teams defaulting to coarse bot mitigation that risks blocking automated customers.

## Why it matters

As autonomous AI agents increasingly conduct research and transactions on the web, enterprises cannot make informed access control or marketing spend decisions while remaining blind to whether traffic is human, crawler, or delegated agent.
