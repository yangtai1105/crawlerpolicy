## Summary

Amazon blocked Meta's Muse shopping agent within 12 days of operation, citing unauthorized access and terms of service violations. The e-commerce platform simultaneously maintains robots.txt blocks against several AI user-agents while launching its own 'Buy for Me' agent. Amazon's internal agent self-identifies in requests and provides an opt-out mechanism for brands.

## Key facts

- Amazon blocked Meta's Muse shopping agent within 12 days of operation.
- Amazon cited unauthorized access and terms of service violations as the justification for blocking Muse.
- Amazon's robots.txt blocks several AI user-agents.
- Amazon launched its own shopping agent named 'Buy for Me'.
- Amazon's 'Buy for Me' agent identifies itself during web interactions.
- Amazon's 'Buy for Me' agent includes a mechanism allowing brands to opt out.

## Insight

Major platforms are using terms of service and crawler controls defensively to exclude third-party consumer agents while replacing them with vertically integrated first-party agentic alternatives.

## Implication

Third-party AI shopping and comparison agents face strict perimeter defenses on dominant marketplaces, forcing agent operators to negotiate direct platform access or risk rapid blocking.

## Why it matters

The development establishes a precedent of platform self-preferencing in the AI agent economy, where marketplaces enforce access controls against external agents while deploying their own proprietary intermediaries.
