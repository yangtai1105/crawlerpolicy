## Summary

Indonesia has introduced a draft copyright bill that would require digital platforms to compensate news publishers for displaying story previews, aggregating articles, and using journalistic content to train AI models. Tech companies including Google, Meta, and TikTok testified in the Indonesian parliament against the proposed measure. Under the framework, payments would be handled by a state-run management entity, with non-compliant platforms risking the cancellation of their local business permits.

## Key facts

- Meta, TikTok, and Google testified in the Indonesian parliament in opposition to a proposed copyright bill.
- The bill requires digital platforms to pay domestic news publishers for surfacing news link previews, story snippets, and using journalism to train AI models.
- The proposed law makes no distinction between news aggregation and republication.
- Compensation would be collected by a state-run management organization and distributed to publishers.
- Platforms that fail to comply could face sanctions, including the loss of their local business permits.
- Berni Moestafa, Meta's head of public policy in Indonesia, testified that if passed, "publishers could lose readership traffic as well as potential advertising and subscription revenue."
- Indonesia has roughly 230 million internet users and was Google's fifth-largest market by user count in 2023 with 4.42 billion searches.

## Insight

The legislation conflates link preview aggregation with full republication and formally couples search index snippets with generative AI model training under a single mandatory collective licensing mandate enforced by local operating permit revocation.

## Implication

If enacted, platforms may consider curtailing news aggregation or blocking news links in Indonesia to avoid mandatory fees, mirroring actions taken after publisher compensation mandates in jurisdictions such as Canada.

## Why it matters

Covering roughly 230 million internet users, Indonesia would become the first Southeast Asian nation to formally integrate AI model training and search snippets into statutory publisher compensation mandates, establishing a regional precedent for platform copyright obligations.
