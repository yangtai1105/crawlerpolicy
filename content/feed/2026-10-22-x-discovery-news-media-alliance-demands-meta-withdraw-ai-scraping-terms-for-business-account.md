## Summary

The News/Media Alliance delivered a letter to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg demanding the withdrawal of a new crawling clause in Meta's Commercial Terms of Use before October 30. The updated terms allow Meta to scrape websites linked to business accounts on its platforms to train business products and AI models. The trade association argues this practice constitutes an anticompetitive exercise of market power that undermines the publisher content licensing market.

## Key facts

- The News/Media Alliance, representing over 2,000 U.S. newspaper, magazine, and digital media publishers, sent a letter to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg demanding the withdrawal of revised Commercial Terms of Use before October 30.
- Meta's updated commercial terms permit the company to scrape content from any website linked to a business account on its platforms to train AI tools and business products.
- Meta made minor revisions to the terms over the weekend, but legal experts state the terms continue to grant broad scraping privileges without explicit limitations.
- The News/Media Alliance stated that "Publishers cannot practically disallow or block Meta’s crawlers without harming their ability to promote content as a business account user."
- The trade group cited an estimate that "Meta already owes U.S. publishers nearly $2 billion a year" for journalism.

## Insight

Platform operators are attempting to leverage social distribution contracts to bypass standard web crawler opt-outs, effectively conditioning social platform presence on granting broad scraping rights for AI training.

## Implication

If Meta enforces the terms on October 30, publishers operating business accounts will either be forced to accept uncompensated AI harvesting or risk forfeiting their social distribution reach.

## Why it matters

Tying platform account access to web scraping consent circumvents traditional crawler access controls, threatening both autonomous publisher opt-out choices and bilateral AI licensing negotiations.
