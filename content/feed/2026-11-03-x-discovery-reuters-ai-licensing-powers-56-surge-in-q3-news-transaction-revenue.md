## Summary

Thomson Reuters reported a 12% revenue increase at Reuters for Q3 2026, driven by a surge in generative AI content licensing deals. Transaction revenue within the news division jumped 56% to $44 million. Across the parent company, generative AI accounted for approximately 34% of underlying contract value.

## Key facts

- Revenue at Reuters rose 12% year-on-year in Q3 2026, driven by a surge in licensing revenue from generative AI.
- Transaction revenue in the Reuters news division increased 56% to $44 million.
- Thomson Reuters reported an overall 10% increase in Q3 revenue to $1.83 billion, lifting its full-year 2026 organic revenue guidance to between 8.5% and 9%.
- Chief financial officer Gary E. Bischoping, Jr. stated that about 34% of the company's underlying contract value relied on generative AI in Q3, up from 32% in Q2 and 24% in 2025.
- In the legal division, operating margins dropped from 49% to 46.6% due to AI investments and $22 million in severance costs.
- Chief executive officer Steve Hasker noted the company expected to announce law firm Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom as a customer for its proprietary AI model, stating: "It will be the first of many."

## Insight

AI licensing is demonstrating measurable, double-digit impacts on news publisher transaction financials, proving that proprietary editorial and archival access deals can serve as tangible commercial lines rather than nominal experiments.

## Implication

Publishers with large, high-integrity informational archives will face increased incentive to wall off open crawling in favor of structured commercial licensing agreements with frontier AI developers.

## Why it matters

Financial disclosures quantifying the direct revenue impact of generative AI content licensing show commercial market clearing prices are emerging for high-value publisher archives.
